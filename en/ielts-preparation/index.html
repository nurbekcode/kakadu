IELTS preparation: Speaking, Writing and mock tests

IELTS has four parts: Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. Speaking and Writing are usually the hardest because they are difficult to check on your own. Kakadu lets you practise exactly those parts every day with AI.

IELTS at a glance

  • Listening — about 30 minutes, 4 parts, 40 questions
  • Reading — 60 minutes, 3 texts, 40 questions
  • Writing — 60 minutes, Task 1 (graph or letter) and Task 2 (essay)
  • Speaking — 11–14 minutes, 3 parts, face-to-face with an examiner

Speaking practice with Kakadu

An AI examiner asks Part 1, Part 2 (cue card) and Part 3 questions. After each answer you get feedback on the four criteria: Fluency and Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy, and Pronunciation. Every mistake is explained in Uzbek or Russian, with a stronger sample answer.

Writing and mock tests

You write Task 1 and Task 2 essays; the AI marks grammar, vocabulary and structure issues and gives an estimated band score. A weekly exam-format mock test shows which part needs the most work.

FAQ

How long does IELTS preparation take?

It depends on your starting level and target score. Moving up one band usually takes a few months of regular practice. Kakadu’s placement test suggests a plan.

Can I practise IELTS Speaking at home?

Yes. On Kakadu you practise with an AI examiner in the real format and get criteria-based feedback after every answer.

Is the Kakadu score an official IELTS result?

No, it is an estimate. Only the official British Council or IDP exam gives an IELTS result.
